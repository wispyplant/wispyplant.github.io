'Outage ＼( ˋ Θ ´ )／' (2026)  is a collection of artworks explore the imagined critter perspectives of environmental monitoring narratives of emerging sound- and vibration-sensing technologies, such as Distributed Acoustic Sensing.


Illustrations (wax and oil pencil colour and watercolour on paper, 42 x 59.4 cm, and wooden puzzle, 41cm diameter), table, mirror, light, thermal printer, Ardunio 

Outage imagines the disruptions of real-time, continuous data collection caused by critter residents near environmental monitoring infrastructures.  

From remote subsea and mountainous areas to populated urban cityscapes, various environments can be monitored with emerging technologies. Distributed Acoustic Sensing (DAS) repurposes unused fibre optic cable – or ‘dark fibre’ – to sense vibrations or sound variations along the cable. Framed as beneficial for ecological protection and multiple other use cases, notably border security monitoring, the use of DAS as a potential ‘smart city’ technology draws questions of consent, data privacy and increased state surveillance.  


The series of illustrations, including a puzzle and thermal prints, are equally inspired by humour in contemporary art comics and spatial documentations of ancient Chinese architectural and cartographic jiehua drawings. While jiehua is now studied retroactively to determine the technical constructions of lost architecture, here it speculates technological infrastructures yet to be built.  

Visualising concealed DAS infrastructures in the environment, the artist draws on her feng shui practice as a form of intuitive and calculative environmental sensing. Mythical beasts are theorised as energetic environmental features that maintain the worldly balance between natural and built landscapes. The critters in Outage are playful extensions of this understanding, embodying a provocation for action by opposing their situation as monitored subjects, to become balancers of the shared ecology.
